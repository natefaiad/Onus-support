Onus User Guide

Onus is a straightforward workout tracker for iPhone and Apple Watch: build programs, log sets in real time, and see your progress and personal records over time — with no account, no ads, and no data leaving your device (except to your own private iCloud, if you choose to back up). This guide walks through the app's main features.

Getting Started

Onus has no sign-up and no account — open the app and start logging. The first time you launch it, you'll be asked to pick one of the built-in preloaded programs to start as your Current program (you can change or ignore this later); after that there's nothing to configure before your first workout. Your data lives on your device (and syncs to your paired Apple Watch).

The app is organized into four tabs: Home, History, Workouts, and Settings. The exercise library lives inside Settings rather than its own tab (see Exercise Library).

Home

Home is your launch pad. From here you can:

  • See your Current Program front and center, with its workouts listed underneath — tap any workout to quick-start it pre-filled with its planned exercises and sets.
  • See any other Active Programs you're running, collapsed below the current one, so you can switch between multiple programs (e.g. alternating a strength block with a conditioning block) without losing track of either.

Starting a Workout

There are two ways to start a session:

  • From your iPhone — quick-start any workout listed on Home, pre-loaded with its planned exercises, sets, reps, and target weights. Only programs marked Active in the Workouts tab show up on Home, so mark a program Active there first to make its workouts available to quick-start. (The Workouts tab itself is for building and editing programs/workouts, not starting them — quick-start happens from Home.)
  • From your Apple Watch — start a workout directly on the Watch, even without your iPhone nearby. Only programs marked Active on iPhone are synced to the Watch, so the same Active programs available to quick-start on Home are what you'll see there (see Apple Watch App).

Only one workout can be in progress at a time. While one is running, the start buttons on Home are hidden, and starting from the other device joins the workout already in progress instead of replacing it.

Logging a Workout

The active workout screen lists each exercise as an expandable card. For each set you log:

  • Weight and Reps, entered on a custom numeric keypad — big digit keys, a decimal point (only where relevant, e.g. disabled for reps), and a dedicated +/- stepper sized per muscle group (see Settings) so you can nudge a value without touching the keyboard. A Next button jumps you straight to the following field.
  • Previous — the weight and reps you logged for that exact set last time, shown right next to the input so progressive overload is a glance away.
  • A Complete checkmark per set — tap it to log the set (and trigger the rest timer). Completing every set in an exercise automatically collapses it and expands the next one (for a superset, both exercises have to be finished first — see below).

You can add or remove exercises and sets mid-workout, and a running elapsed time is always visible in the toolbar. When you're done, Finish Workout saves it to your history; Discard Workout throws it away.

Swipe the workout down to get back to the rest of the app. A banner at the bottom of the screen keeps showing the workout name, the exercise and set you're on, and any running timer. Tap it to jump back in.

If a workout was started from a template and you changed the plan on the fly (added/removed an exercise or set, changed a target), Onus shows a Template Changes review after you finish, listing what changed by exercise so you can accept the changes into the template or reject them and keep the template as-is.

Supersets

A Superset pairs two exercises to perform back-to-back, at your own pace. To create one, open a workout's options menu, choose Add Superset, select two exercises, and tap Group as Superset. (The option needs at least two standalone exercises in the workout.)

Each superset has its own name, a Sets count, and a Rests duration taken after each round. During a workout, the superset's card shows both exercises and only moves on once both have completed the current round. If you remove one exercise from a superset, the other goes back to being a regular standalone exercise.

Circuit Workouts

A Circuit turns a whole workout into a timed interval session: Onus runs the clock and moves you from exercise to exercise. To set one up, open the workout's options menu and choose Convert Workout to Circuit. Every exercise joins one circuit (any supersets are merged into it), and exercises you add later join it automatically. To go back, choose Convert Workout to Standard, which removes the timing and makes every exercise standalone again.

A circuit has four settings:

  • Sets — how many rounds to run. Every exercise in the circuit always has the same number of sets.
  • Work — how long each exercise's interval lasts (60 seconds by default).
  • Transition — rest between exercises within a round (15 seconds by default).
  • Recovery — rest after a full round, before the next one starts (your Default Duration rest by default).

The −/+ buttons change these by your Rest Timer → Increment setting. You can rename the circuit and reorder its exercises from its header menu.

Running a circuit. On iPhone, tap Start Circuit to begin the first work interval. On Apple Watch, a 5-second "Starting in" countdown shows which exercise is up first. The circuit then runs round by round: set 1 of every exercise, then set 2, and so on. The timer shows Now: during work and Next: during rests, plus where you are in the round. Work intervals are shown in gold and rests in teal. The controls are Pause, −/+ (adjust the current phase by your Increment), and Skip (end the current phase now; skipping a work interval completes that set). If you change the Work time mid-session, it applies to the next interval.

Logging reps. Since the timer finishes each set for you, reps are confirmed afterwards. A set whose interval has ended is waiting to log, shown with its values and checkmark in gold. Onus pre-fills a suggestion from your previous round, the template's target, or your last session. Tap the check to log it as-is, or edit the values first. On Apple Watch, the To Log page steps through the waiting sets with the ‹ and › buttons. If you tap Finish Workout with sets still unlogged, Onus offers to take you back to log them.

Automatic pause. So you don't miss an interval while you're entering numbers, a circuit pauses on its own at the next phase change when you're typing on iPhone, when you're on the To Log page on the Watch, or when you've swiped the workout down to the banner. If you've left Onus, you'll get a Circuit Paused notification. Press play to pick up where you left off.

Circuit sets don't count toward Personal Records, since timed intervals aren't comparable to regular sets. Onus ships with a preloaded Spartacus Circuit program if you want to try one out.

Rest Timer

In a standard workout, completing a set automatically starts a rest timer (except after the last set). It uses the exercise's own rest time, or your Default Duration if it doesn't have one. The timer shows what's coming next (the exercise and set number) and has Pause, −/+, and Skip controls. The −/+ step is set in Settings → Rest Timer → Increment. It keeps counting even if you background the app, alerting you with your chosen sound when it ends.

Personal Records

Onus automatically detects when a completed set is a new personal record for that exercise and celebrates it with an on-screen trophy animation. PR sets are marked with a trophy badge everywhere they appear — the active workout, workout summaries, history, and Progress Graphs (where PR points are drawn larger and in a distinct color) — so your best lifts are always easy to spot. Sets logged during a circuit aren't counted as PRs.

Workouts & Programs

The Workouts tab has two sub-tabs:

  • Programs — a program groups related workouts together (e.g. a Push/Pull/Legs split or an Upper/Lower split). Mark a program Active to make it available to quick-start, and mark one Current to feature it on Home. Onus ships with several preloaded programs; you can choose which ones appear in your library from Settings → Programs → Preloaded Programs.
  • Workouts — individual workout templates (e.g. "Push Day"), each a list of exercises with planned sets, target reps, target weight, and a rest timer duration per exercise. Workouts can belong to one or more programs, or stand alone.

Both programs and workouts are fully editable — reorder exercises and sets, pair exercises into supersets, turn a workout into a circuit, adjust targets, or delete what you no longer need. When adding workouts to a program, the confirm button shows how many you've selected (e.g. Add (3)). If you try to close an editor with unsaved edits, Onus asks whether to Discard Changes or Keep Editing.

Copying. To start from something you already have, tap the add button on either sub-tab and choose Copy Existing, then pick the program or workout to copy. The copy opens in the editor named "<original> (Copy)", ready to rename, and nothing is saved until you tap Save.

  • Copying a workout copies everything: notes, exercises, sets and targets, rest times, and supersets/circuits. The new workout stands alone and isn't added to any program.
  • Copying a program keeps its workouts in the same order (but not its Active/Current status). By default the copy shares the original's workouts, so editing one updates both. Turn on Duplicate Workout Templates to give the new program its own independent copies of each workout instead. You can name each copy before saving.

Exercise Library

Browse the full built-in exercise catalog from Settings → Exercises → Exercise Library, filterable by muscle group, equipment type, and search text. Each exercise entry shows its primary and secondary muscles and equipment, and has a lookup menu for form instructions via YouTube, muscleandstrength.com, musclewiki.com, or the free-exercise-db project — opened in an in-app browser so you never leave Onus.

Muscle groups are Chest, Back, Shoulders, Biceps, Triceps, Forearms, Core, Quads, Hamstrings, Glutes, Calves, Full Body, Cardio, and Other.

You can also add your own custom exercises with a name, primary muscle, any number of secondary muscles, and equipment type — useful for movements not in the built-in catalog or gym equipment that's a little different from the standard. Secondary muscles appear in brackets after the primary one, e.g. "Chest (Triceps, Shoulders)". Filters and progress use the primary muscle. To change or remove a custom exercise later, swipe left on it in the library and tap Edit or Delete. When adding exercises to a workout, you can multi-select several at once instead of adding them one at a time.

History

Every completed workout is logged with:

  • A weekly summary — workouts completed, total volume, and average duration for the current week.
  • A chronologically grouped list of past sessions.
  • A detail view per session: duration, total volume, exercise count, and (if you use the Watch) average heart rate and calories burned, followed by a full set-by-set breakdown with PR badges.

History has a Progress sub-view alongside the chronological list — see Progress Graphs below. You can also set a Start Date in Settings to limit how far back both views look.

Progress Graphs

Track how an exercise is trending over time as a line chart, from History → Progress. Tap Add Graph to pick any exercise — from a program, a specific workout, or your other logged exercises — and Onus plots it going forward.

Each graph can show 1RM Estimate, Top Set, Volume, or Max Reps, over the last 30, 60, or 90 days or All Time, with an optional trend line. PR sets stand out as larger, highlighted points. Use Filter Graphs to narrow which graphs are shown and Manage Graphs to bulk-edit or delete them. If you'd rather not manage graphs by hand, turn on Only Show Current Program in Settings and Onus keeps your graphs in sync with your Current program's exercises automatically.

Apple Watch App

Onus runs as a standalone Apple Watch app — workouts can be started, logged, and finished entirely from the wrist, even without the iPhone nearby.

  • Home on the Watch mirrors your active programs and workouts from the phone, so you can quick-start the same way you would on iPhone.
  • Active workout shows the current exercise, a segmented progress bar for its sets (teal for done, pulsing for the current set, red for skipped), and REPS and LB/KG rows with −/+ buttons. Reps change by 1 and weight by the exercise's increment. You can also turn the Digital Crown to adjust whichever value is outlined, or tap the outlined value again to type an exact number on a keypad. Buttons along the bottom step to the previous or next set and complete the current one.
  • In a standard workout, the rest timer appears on the same screen, with −/+ around the countdown and Pause and Skip below it, plus what's up next. Circuit workouts get a dedicated interval timer page, and the set screen becomes the To Log page (see Circuit Workouts).
  • Firm haptic taps let you know when to lift: three buzzes when rest ends, two when a circuit work interval ends, and a tick for each of the last three seconds of a circuit interval.
  • Swipe over to the Now Playing screen to control music without leaving the workout.
  • A summary screen shows elapsed time, your live heart rate, and active calories at the top, followed by every exercise and set for the in-progress workout. You can add or remove sets and jump directly to any set. It flags whether you finished every set or left some incomplete, and if you tap Finish Workout with sets still open, Onus double-checks that you meant to before wrapping up.
  • Working out with both devices. A workout started on either iPhone or Apple Watch joins the other automatically whenever they're in range, and both stay in sync while you train. Log a set on one and it shows up on the other, the timer matches on both, and the iPhone shows your Watch's live heart rate and calories. If both devices change the same set at once, the most recent change wins. Finishing or discarding on either device ends the workout on both.
  • Workouts done only on the Watch sync back to iPhone automatically via WatchConnectivity — no internet connection involved, just a direct device-to-device link — and, just like on iPhone, you'll get a chance to review and accept any changes back into the template.
  • A watch face complication gives you a one-tap shortcut straight into Onus.

Settings

Onus is tuned to how you train:

  • Appearance — light, dark, or system.
  • Exercises — default source for the exercise library's form-lookup shortcut (YouTube, etc.), and access to the Exercise Library.
  • Rest Timer — Default Duration, Increment (how much the −/+ timer buttons and circuit settings change by: 5, 10, 15, 30, or 60 seconds), and completion Sound.
  • Previous Set — show your last performance for the same workout only, or for any workout that used the same exercise.
  • Weight — pounds or kilograms (switching doesn't touch your stored data, only how it's displayed), and whether dumbbell sets log the weight of one dumbbell (Onus doubles it for volume) or the combined weight of both.
  • Weight Increment — separate +/- step sizes for lower body, upper body, and isolation movements, so the quick-adjust buttons match how you actually load a bar or pick a dumbbell.
  • Programs — choose which preloaded programs appear in your library.
  • History — a Start Date cutoff for History and Progress, plus default metric, date range, and trend-line settings for new Progress Graphs.
  • Import/Export Data — see below.

Data Export, Import & iCloud Backup

From Settings you can:

  • Export Workout History as a CSV — every completed set across every session, ready for a spreadsheet.
  • Export Programs & Workouts as a JSON file you can share (e.g. AirDrop it to a friend or training partner) so they can preview and selectively import your programs and workout templates.
  • Import Programs & Workouts from a file someone shared with you the same way — you'll see a preview before anything is added.
  • iCloud Backup — Onus automatically saves a private snapshot of all your data (programs, workouts, and history) to your own iCloud Drive after workouts and when you leave the app, so it can be recovered if you lose or replace your device. You can also back up or restore manually; restoring only adds data and never deletes anything already on your device. See the Privacy Policy for details on what this backup contains and where it goes.

Apple Health Integration

With permission, Onus reads heart rate, active energy burned, and body mass (the last only to estimate calories when the Watch doesn't supply a measured value) to enrich your workout summaries. Every completed workout is also written to Apple Health so it shows up alongside your other activity. Workouts tracked with the Apple Watch are saved by the Watch with measured heart rate and calories. Workouts logged from iPhone alone are saved by the iPhone with estimated calories. A workout is never saved twice when you use both devices. Strength workouts are saved as Traditional Strength Training, and circuit workouts as High Intensity Interval Training. This is all handled by Apple's HealthKit framework directly on your device — see the Privacy Policy for details.

Privacy

Onus doesn't collect, transmit, or sell any of your data — full details are in the Privacy Policy.
